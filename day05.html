What is phishing?

Phishing is social engineering: an attacker impersonates a trusted party to trick you into handing over credentials, money, or access — or into running malware. It works because it exploits people, not software. Three levers do most of the work:

🎭

Impersonation

Looks like your bank, IT team, a colleague, or a brand you trust — so you lower your guard.

⏰

Urgency & fear

"Your account will be closed", "unpaid invoice", "suspicious login" — pressure to act before you think.

🪤

A single action

Click a link, open an attachment, approve an MFA prompt, or reply with information. One slip is enough.

URL inspector — read the real destination

The single most useful phishing skill: finding the registered domain in a link. That's the part right before the first single slash — everything else is decoration attackers use to fool you. Try the examples (all fictional) or paste your own.

💡
Rule of thumb: read a URL right-to-left from the first single "/". The domain immediately before it is who really controls the page — login.microsoft.com.evil.ru is controlled by evil.ru, not Microsoft.

How to defend

Because phishing targets people, defense is part human, part technical.

🔑
Phishing-resistant MFA matters. Even a stolen password fails if login needs a hardware security key or passkey (FIDO2/WebAuthn). SMS codes and push approvals can still be phished in real time by attacker-in-the-middle kits — hardware-bound keys can't.
