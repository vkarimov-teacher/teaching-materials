The OSI & TCP/IP Models

Layered models split networking into jobs, so each layer only talks to its neighbors. Click any layer to see what it does, its protocols, and how it's attacked.

📦 Encapsulation — the mailroom analogy

As data goes down the stack on the sender, each layer wraps it in its own header (like nesting envelopes): data → segment (L4) → packet (L3) → frame (L2) → bits (L1). The receiver unwraps them in reverse. That's why a layer only needs to understand its own header.

Network Security

Every defense maps back to three goals — the CIA triad. Click a threat card to see the fix.

C
Confidentiality

Only authorized parties can read the data. Broken by sniffing & MITM. Fixed by encryption.

I
Integrity

Data isn't altered in transit. Broken by tampering & spoofing. Fixed by hashing & signatures.

A
Availability

Service stays reachable. Broken by DoS/DDoS. Fixed by rate-limiting & redundancy.

🔐 Defense in depth

No single control is enough. Layer them — perimeter firewall, segmented internal zones, encrypted transport, endpoint protection, monitoring, and least-privilege access — so an attacker who beats one control still faces the next.
