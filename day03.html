concept

What is a denial-of-service attack?

A DoS (Denial of Service) attack tries to make a system unavailable to its legitimate users — it targets the Availability leg of the CIA triad. Instead of stealing data, it exhausts a limited resource: bandwidth, connection slots, CPU, memory, or an application's worker threads.

🎯

DoS — one source

A single machine floods the target. Easier to trace and block by its source address.

🌐

DDoS — many sources

Thousands of compromised devices (a botnet) attack at once. Far harder to filter, since traffic comes from everywhere.

📡

Reflection & amplification

The attacker spoofs the victim's IP and asks public servers for large replies — turning a small request into a huge flood aimed at the victim.

Attacks fall into three families by what they exhaust. That's the map for both the taxonomy below and the Defense Lab — because the right mitigation depends entirely on the family.
